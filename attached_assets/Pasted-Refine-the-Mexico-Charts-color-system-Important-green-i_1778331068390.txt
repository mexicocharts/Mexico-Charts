Refine the Mexico Charts color system.

Important: green is the primary brand color, but DO NOT make everything green.

The goal is not “more green.”
The goal is a more controlled, premium palette where green feels powerful because the rest of the site is restrained.

Keep the core palette:

* Obsidian black / near-black backgrounds
* White typography
* Muted gray secondary text
* Electric emerald green as the primary brand accent
* Deep purple only as a subtle atmospheric secondary color

Reduce the number of competing colors.

Current issue:
Some sections use too many saturated colors at once, which makes the site feel slightly too busy/gaming-like.

Fix:

* Desaturate strong orange, cyan, red, magenta, and blue areas.
* Replace bright rainbow-style cards with darker cinematic tones.
* Use green for highlights, active states, key numbers, CTAs, ticker, borders, and important accents.
* Use purple only for depth, atmosphere, hero glow, or subtle background gradients.
* Keep colored variety only where it helps differentiate content, but make those colors darker, smokier, and less saturated.

Do NOT:

* Turn every card green.
* Make the whole site monochrome green.
* Remove all color personality.
* Make it look flat or boring.
* Overuse green glows everywhere.
* Make it look like crypto, gaming, or esports.

Specific direction:

* Genre cards can still have different moods, but they should feel like dark cinematic territories, not bright colorful blocks.
* Touring cards should feel like stage lighting: dark, premium, subtle, with controlled green accents.
* Artist cards can keep photography/color from the images, but UI overlays should stay mostly black/gray/white/green.
* Data visualizations should prioritize green intensity, opacity, and line weight instead of random rainbow colors.
* Buttons should mostly use green or dark glass styles.
* Keep the site feeling expensive, editorial, and music-culture focused.

Target feeling:
Black and green should be the recognizable Mexico Charts identity.
Purple should add atmosphere.
Other colors should be rare, muted, and supportive.

The final result should feel more premium, less busy, less rainbow, and more brand-owned — without becoming “everything green.”
